Keystone02 / 17
THE PROBLEM

Fully on-chain order books have been economically impossible on EVM

The Loop

Every quote is a transaction

Place, cancel and match each cost gas. A maker refreshing quotes hundreds of times a day pays for every one of them.

The Cost

Volatile gas kills the maker

Cancel-replace economics break the moment fees spike unpredictably. Spreads widen to cover gas risk, or makers simply leave.

The Outcome

Serious books went off-chain

dYdX-style venues moved matching to private servers. On-chain matching became a thing you apologise for.

The one great counterexample — DeepBook on Sui — proved native CLOBs work when execution is sub-second and fees are negligible and predictable. That combination had never existed on an EVM chain.

PRODUCT — THE BOOK

A matching engine that survives a block gas limit

◆ Price-time priority — sorted price-level linked list, FIFO within each level

◆ Bounded matching — MAX_MATCHES = 20 fills per transaction, partial-fill rest and resume

◆ Caller-supplied insertion hint, bounded fallback scan (MAX_HINT_SCAN = 10)

◆ Order flags — FLAG_POST_ONLY, FLAG_IOC, plus slippage bounds

◆ No external calls mid-match — settlement is pure internal debit/credit through BalanceManager

The risk bound doing its job

After that fill the Reserve sat ~88.6% quote-heavy — past the 80% bound implied by maxInventorySkewBps = 3000. Its next ask reverted with InventorySkewExceeded(). The bid side, which reduces skew, placed fine.

Not a bug report — the feature. On-chain bounds refusing a quote that would deepen an imbalance, on real capital.

Vault liquidity

Quotes the Book on the vault's behalf, harvests fees into NAV. Tighter to mid than either bot — and the order the live market sell filled against.

Reference-Feed

Real signal

Pushes the live EUR/USD rate from the ECB via Frankfurter on-chain — 1.1435, replacing the hardcoded 1.08 placeholder.

An order book produces prices; it does not consume them. KeystoneBook never reads the oracle — the reference mid has exactly two consumers, both outside matching: the quoting agents, and the Reserve's NAV math.

How value flows

Takers pay 10 bps; makers earn a 2 bps rebate funded out of that fee. The net spread flows to whichever side made the market — including Keystone Reserve, whose depositors earn it as real, on-chain NAV growth.

Every pair, same mechanism

No separate token, no emissions. Revenue scales with real trading volume across every future stablecoin corridor Arc lists — BRLA, JPYC, MXNB, and beyond.
